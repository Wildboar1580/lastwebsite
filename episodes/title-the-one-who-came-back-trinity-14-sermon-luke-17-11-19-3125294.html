Episode Description

Ten lepers cry out to Jesus for mercy. Ten hear His Word. Ten believe His promise and go on their way to the priests. Ten are cleansed.

But only one comes back.

The one who returns is the least likely of them all—a Samaritan, a foreigner, and a leper. The other nine receive the earthly blessing they wanted and continue on their way. The Samaritan returns to Christ, falls at His feet, and gives thanks.

Their cleansing was not defective. Their faith had not been false simply because they later fell away. But earthly blessings became enough for the nine. They wanted to be clean, restored to society, and accepted under the outward order of the Law. Only the Samaritan continued to cling to Christ.

So it remains in the Church. Many receive Baptism, hear God's Word, and come to the Lord's Supper, yet become satisfied with earthly blessings, outward church membership, ancestry, respectability, or their own works. Christ's true Church remains a little flock, often found where the world and even the visible Church least expect it.

The truth has never been popular. The descendants of those who killed the prophets built monuments to them. Men praise Athanasius, Luther, and Walther after they are safely dead while rejecting the Word for which they contended. If Christ Himself appeared among many who bear His name, they would cast Him out for saying the same things He says in Scripture.

Yet among ten cleansed lepers, faith endures in the Samaritan outcast.

“Rise and go your way; your faith has saved you.”

A Trinity 14 sermon on Luke 17:11–19 about faith, thanksgiving, the little flock, and clinging to Christ rather than our works, ancestry, church membership, or earthly blessings.
