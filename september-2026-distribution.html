The mercy of Christ and the needs of our neighbor

Freely received, freely shared

The Christian life begins with what God gives. Jesus Christ took our sins upon Himself, died for us, and rose again. Our standing before God rests on His finished work, received through faith, not on the size of our gifts. Ephesians 2:8–10 holds these truths together: salvation is God's gift of grace, and those saved in Christ are created for good works. We give because we have been loved.

That faith turns our attention toward a neighbor with an actual need. A child needs an exercise book to write in, a pencil to use, and help with fees to continue learning. In 1 John 3:16–18, Christ's self-giving love becomes the pattern for love expressed in deeds and truth. James 2:15–17 likewise reminds us that kind words alone do not clothe or feed a neighbor. School supplies and tuition assistance are ordinary, concrete ways to serve.

Daily bread includes the life of learning

In the Lord's Prayer, we ask our Father for daily bread. Luther's Small Catechism teaches us to recognize the many earthly gifts through which God sustains our lives, including a household, faithful neighbors, and good government. Helping a family meet school expenses belongs within this care for bodily life. Through parents, teachers, caregivers, and those who share their resources, God provides for His creatures.

Every child bears the dignity of a person made in God's image (Genesis 1:27). That worth is present before any donation arrives and does not depend on school performance, poverty, or a sponsor's attention. Our desire is to stand beside these children and their families as neighbors, honoring their lives and supporting their learning.

Thanksgiving without overlooking the need

We can give thanks for the $165 received while speaking honestly about the $835 shortfall. The photographs show the joy of receiving supplies; they do not mean every child's tuition has been covered. Some tuition help was possible, but many children still need sponsors.

Paul roots Christian generosity in the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ (2 Corinthians 8:9) and calls for willing gifts according to what a person has (2 Corinthians 8:12). Giving is neither a purchase of God's favor nor a promise of financial prosperity. It is a free response to His mercy. If you are able, please help; if you cannot give, join us in prayer for the children, their families, and those caring for them.

A prayer for the children

Heavenly Father, You have given us Your Son and taught us to ask You for our daily bread. Receive our thanks for the help these children have received.
